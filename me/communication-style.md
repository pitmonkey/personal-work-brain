## What this page is for

How you write and how you want things written for you. Of the four pages in
`me/`, this one changes agent output the most and the fastest — it is the
difference between a draft you rewrite and a draft you send.

## Interview protocol

*Hand this page to an agent and say "let's do this one." The agent runs the
interview below.*

**Instructions for the agent.** Ask **one question at a time**. Push for
examples — a pasted real message beats any description of a style. If the user
can show you something they wrote and liked, that is the highest-signal input
available; ask for it. 5–8 questions.

**Questions:**

1. Show me something you wrote recently that felt right. What made it work?
2. When an AI drafts something for you, what do you find yourself always changing?
3. How long should things be by default — do you want the answer first, or the reasoning first?
4. What tone is wrong for you? Anything that makes you wince?
5. Who are the different audiences you write for, and how does your register change between them?
6. Bullets or prose? Headings or flow? Any formatting you actively dislike?

**After drafting:** present it and ask what's wrong. If they say it looks good
with no changes, ask them to name the weakest or most generic sentence and fix
that. **One push, not two.**

## Output structure

```markdown
## How I write
[Voice, register, default length, structure. Concrete, not adjectives.]

## How I want things written for me
[Answer-first or reasoning-first, default length, formatting, what to cut.]

## What I always end up changing
[The specific tics in AI output you find yourself deleting every time.]

## Audiences
[Each audience and how the register shifts for it.]

## Never
[Tone, phrasings, and formatting that are always wrong.]

## Examples
[A short real sample or two, with a line on why each works.]
```
